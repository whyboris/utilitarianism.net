## And We Need Your Contribution!

{{< chinese-translation-callout >}}

_An open initiative arising from a panel at ISUS 18, University of Macau, July 2026_

In Chinese, utilitarianism is standardly translated as {{< chinese >}}, a term which also means vices or attitudes such as “short-sightedness”, “egoism”/”self-interested”, material seeking/metric seeking/money seeking, in ordinary and daily conversational Chinese. The authors of this article thought this was rather unfortunate and had negative consequences, and have discussed the issue of this translation for more than two years.

We raised the issue during a panel at the 18th conference of the International Society for Utilitarian Studies (University of Macau, July 2026), where the proposal to reconsider {{< chinese >}} as the Chinese translation of utilitarianism met with near-unanimous support. We discussed with a few engaged stakeholders and decided to launch an initiative: Its aim is first to discuss the issue at hand, and then to form a consensus recommendation.

With gratitude to the owners of _utilitarianism.net_, **we announce that the English-language discussions for this topic will be conducted on a sub-page on** _utilitarianism.net_.

**We will launch a discussion forum** for scholars to discuss this issue. We have not decided yet on the launch date, but we will make an announcement ASAP. Before that, we will upload materials that lay the foundation for this discussion, including a detailed introductory article on the issue and the papers presented at the workshop.

## Introduction to the Initiative

This initiative formally began with a panel at the 18th conference of the International Society for Utilitarian Studies (University of Macau, July 2026), organized by Zhai Xiaobo and Tse Yip Fai, and chaired by Peter Singer. We invited both Chinese speakers and non-Chinese speakers, to discuss whether we should change {{< chinese >}} into some other terms, as a Chinese translation of the word “utilitarianism”. There were almost unanimous (one participant strongly objected it) agreement that the current Chinese translation is unideal or very unideal; and almost unanimous (again the same participant strong objected it) support for the proposal that we should at least discuss a potential change of the translation. And a majority of them were willing to consider contributing to an initiative to change the translation. After the workshop, we had a series of discussions with a few of the more engaged stakeholders about launching an initiative to discuss a potential change, which led to a formal launch of an initiative to discuss a potential change.

We drew a rough roadmap of the initiative, and it has five stages, and we are just past the second, moving toward the third.

1. Gathering and writing academic work that pointed out issues about the translation, and argument for changing the translation. You can see Peter Singer’s article on this issue [here](introduction-to-the-issue), and a deep analysis of the issue [here](reconsider-the-chinese-translation). Some of these work were presented at the workshop at ISUS Macau, which will be uploaded to this page later. They include:

    - A study by Tse Yip Fai experimenting how AI models are affected negatively by the current Chinese translation of utilitarianism.

    - A paper by Daisuke Nakai on how utilitarianism were introduced to Japan, and acquired the translation功利主义 (pronounced differently in Japan, but are essentially using the same characters as the Chinese translation of utilitarianism)

    - A paper by Yang Tianjiang, on reconsidering the Chinese translation of utilitarianism, and five possible alternative translations.

    - A paper by Zhai Xiaobo and Tse Yip Fai on arguments why we should change the current Chinese translation of utilitarianism.
2. Gather a preliminary, but broad-based support within the utilitarian community about the need for a wider discussion about a potential change: That’s what the workshop at ISUS Macau did.
3. Discuss whether we should change the Chinese translation of Utilitarianism: That is what the forum under this sub-page, and the WeChat account, will do. (links to the forums to be announced)
4. Next steps, to be decided later, as the discussions proceed.

In the next phase, phase 3 of the initiative, we ask you to discuss whether the standard translation should change. So gather your thoughts, submit your starting forum posts to us (link to our emails), and stay tuned for the forum!
