Prefix Literal sense Who has used it, and where it stands
效益xiàoyì literally "effectiveness" + “benefit”
  • Now dominant in academic philosophy in Taiwan.
  • It is hard to trace who first raised this alternative, and when. But some earlier adopters who can be traced include ：林火旺 (Lin Huo-wang, Professor at the Philosophy Department of the National Taiwan University) and 钱永祥 (Sechin Y. S. Chien, Corresponding Research Fellow, Institute of History and Philology, Academia Sinica)
  • The Taiwan edition of Mill's Utilitarianism is 《效益主义》, and its publisher gives our reason verbatim: “Since the word 功利 in Chinese can be easily misunderstood as seeking self-interest or profit-seeking, I changed it into 效益”. (「因『功利』的中文一词易遭误解，误以为是追求私利或唯利是图，故后来以『效益』代之。」)
效用xiàoyòng also one of the common translations of "utility" as an economics terminology
  • Not popular anywhere, but used in the recently published translated version of Utilitarianism: A Very Short Introduction
    • Tse Yip Fai, Peter Singer, and Katarzyna de Lazari-Radek, wrote a special preface for the translated book, on the topic of the translation issue. You can view it here [English version URL; Chinese version URL]
  • Reproduces the English structure exactly, since 效用 is the economics term. On the diagnosis above, this is arguably the most faithful option; it is currently the least discussed.
功用gōngyòng "function, use"
  • Used by 唐钺 (Tang Yue), in his translation of Mill’s Utilitarianism, in 1957.
  • The headword on Cantonese Wikipedia.
乐利lèlì literally "happiness” + “benefit"
  • Used by 梁启超 (Liang Qichao), in 《乐利主义泰斗边沁之学说》published in 1902. Note that Liang later turned back to using 功利主义 (gōnglì zhǔyì)
最大幸福 "greatest-happiness", plus “–ism” is literally “greatest-happiness-ism"
